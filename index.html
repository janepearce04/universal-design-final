What is accesible media?

Hello all! My name is Jane and I want to tell you about web accessibility. Web accessibility is the design of digital content that has been modified to be perceivable and understandable by those affected by disabilities. Did you know that almost 1/7 of earth’s population in humans struggles with cognitive or physical impairments? That’s more than the amount of wildlife left on the planet! And while many with some kind of physical or cognitive impairment deal with plenty from a day to day basis offline, the web is no exception as a good 82% of websites fail to meet accessibility and legibility standards. Why do we have these standards anyways?

Looking back at the creation of the web and websites, things were not always built this way. Rules and expectations were formed on the internet because for a while, accessibility wasn’t considered when creating websites, making it impossible for anybody that struggles with accessibility barred from trying to make sense of the web. Additionally, different browsers and websites would have different rules and it was all too difficult and impossible to make cohesive and coordinated, which enabled the introduction of W3c. This has lead on to make accessibility in webpage development a strong topic of discussion and advocacy, onwards to the creation of the web content accessibility guidelines or WCAG.

Sure, there are pathways to doing this most efficiently in the virtual infrastructure we’ve built, but as long as you can get the point across, and it is conformant to the standards of accessibility and effective communication, that’s all that matters. Even this becomes an issue for those with accessibility issues as a good 50% of websites don’t include accessible instructions for complex tasks. Now imagine trying to complete something required for a work conference without being able to see, nor have any instruction on what to do. Without an inclusive enough framework to be able to catch any demographic with accessibility issues, your website will not be an effective resource for clients of different backgrounds.

Accessibility testing can be a good way of gauging as to whether your webpage is lacking in certain areas and is formatted correctly for accessibility.

So what actually are problems that web users with accessibility might face? Of the large population of humans that struggle with some kind of impairment, visual disabilities are likely the most common. However, some are just the inverse and rely on visual cues and communication to make up for a lack of hearing - which is labelling things in a website can be so challenging for those that don’t understand the makeup of creating an accessible website. Amongst other impairments, like motor impairments that make it difficult to operate a keyboard, or cognitive and learning impairments that affect one’s ability to interpret the webpage, and neurological impairments are no exception. The importance of being able to still use a webpage despite these limitations are what set apart a good website from a great website.
